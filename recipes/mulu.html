Mulukhiyah

One of egypt most famous dishes

Mulukhiyah (also spelled molokhia or jute mallow) is a traditional Middle Eastern and North African dish made from finely chopped jute leaves simmered in a rich, savory broth. Celebrated for its vibrant green appearance and signature smooth, velvety texture, the stew is finished with a fragrant ta'leya-a sizzling temper of golden minced garlic and ground coriander bloomed in ghee or butter. Deeply comforting and earthy in flavor, it is traditionally served over fluffy white rice with roasted chicken, rabbit, or warm flatbread, making it one of the region's most iconic home-cooked meals.

ingredients

  • Jute
  • beef or chicken

steps for preparation

  1. Add mulukhiyah over stock and chicken, stir everything well together. Once it starts boiling turn it to medium-low let simmer. To prevent fresh mulukhiyah from turning slimy, add one tomato chopped in half (tomato only if using fresh mulukhiyah, toss before serving).
  2. Let the mix simmer about half an hour, add salt and allspice.
  3. While mulukhiyah is simmering, place olive oil and gee in a skillet. Add crushed garlic, and sauté for a couple of minutes until garlic just turns light golden brown. Add this mixture to the mulukhiyah stew, and stir thoroughly. Taste for seasoning and adjust to liking.
  4. If you like the mulukhiyah soupier, you can add a little more broth. Serve with rice and lots of lemon juice on the side. Some like to add chopped onions dipped in vinegar in lieu of fresh lemon juice. Mulukhiyeh often pairs well with radishes and green peppers on the side.
  5. If you have frozen mulukhiyah you can follow step 1 and it doesn't need to be defrosted.
